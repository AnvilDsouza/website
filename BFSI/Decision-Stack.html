The Decision Stack: BFSI Architecture

The hierarchical model showing how 5 decision primitives build the entire financial institution's intelligence and systems.

Level 5: Institutional Intelligence

Top Layer / AI Synthesis

Here, AI, Rules Engines, and Risk Engines operate *across* systems. Institutional behavior (Explainability, Consistency) emerges here.

AI for Identity Resolution
AI for Risk Prediction
Rules for Product Eligibility
AI Value: Provides the synthesis, speed, and continuous learning for competitive advantage.

Level 4: Decision Systems

System Level

Decision Flows interconnect to form major systems like Onboarding, Lending, or Surveillance. This level's stability depends entirely on the primitives below it.

Level 3: Decision Flows

Process Level

A sequence of dependent decisions forming a process (e.g., KYC Remediation). It is the architecture of the process, built from Decision Objects.

Level 2: Decision Objects

Request Level

A structured bundle of primitives used to evaluate a single request: "Execute this trade," "Approve this loan." This is what systems manipulate.

Example: Credit Approval = Identity + Risk + Eligibility + Entitlement + Escalation Schema

Level 1: Decision Primitives (Atomic Units)

Foundation

The non-negotiable, universally present building blocks of every decision. Consistency here ensures consistency everywhere.

Identity (Who)
Risk (How Risky)
Eligibility (Allowed?)
Entitlement (Permitted Scope)
Escalation (When Unsure)
Human Loop: Defines the governance, policy, and mandate (e.g., Risk Appetite) for each primitive's logic.

Architect's Commentary: Value of Governing Primitives

  • Prevents Failure: If Identity is unstable at Level 1, every system (Level 4) and every AI model (Level 5) built upon it will ultimately fail.
  • Enables AI/ML: AI/ML operates at scale (Levels 2 & 5). It requires Level 1 primitives to be Standardised and Explainable to ensure model accuracy and compliance.
  • Accountability: This model provides the clear language for leaders to mandate governance ("We need a single definition for Risk across all systems," not "We need a new risk system").
